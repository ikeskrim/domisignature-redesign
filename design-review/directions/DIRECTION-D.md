## Three lines of rationale

1. **The voyage works, and it is the real prize here.** Arrival → the six steps
   → the four venues → the enquiry gives the homepage a spine the noir build
   does not have. The "How it works" register makes the Wedding Journey feel
   like an operator's process rather than a marketing timeline.
2. **Facts as luxury is the strongest single move.** `04 / 20–300 / 06` set at
   7vw does more selling than any adjective we have written in six phases, and
   the coordinates lifted from the real map embeds make the venues read as
   places on a map rather than listings.
3. **The light ground is a genuine trade, not an upgrade.** Warm white makes the
   photography friendlier and the interface more contemporary — but our best
   photographs are dusk and night, and they were engineered for exactly the
   opposite ground.

## What is lost from noir — honestly

- **The brand thread.** D's hero says "Crete", not "Domisignature", and drops
  *Where Every Moment Is Signed* from the first screen. White Desert can do this
  because "Antarctica" *is* their product. Our product is a planner, and the
  tagline is the thread you asked to run through the whole site. In the hero
  side-by-side this is the clearest difference and, to me, the clearest loss.
- **The photography's best register.** Compare `compare-1440-1-hero.png`: the
  dusk aerial is a night photograph. On noir it glows. On warm white it has to
  be hazed into the page to stay legible, which costs it contrast and drama.
- **Distinctiveness.** Playfair at hero scale over a dark frame does not look
  like other wedding sites. Geometric sans on warm white is the current house
  style of a great many luxury studios — it is more fashionable and less
  singular.

## What is gained

- **Structure**: the voyage, the six-step register, and the two-CTA system
  (Enquire primary, Wedding Brochure secondary) — visible on the first screen
  rather than buried.
- **Numbers**: stat typography, which we already had the raw material for and
  were not using.
- **Coordinates**: real annotation, zero fabrication, immediate credibility.
- **Legibility on long reads.** Dark grounds are harder for venue prose and the
  wedding guide; D removes that problem entirely.

---

## My recommendation

**Stay noir — fold in the structure.**

Every gain above is structural: the voyage order, the stats typography, the
coordinates, the "How it works" register, and the two-CTA system. **None of them
require the light ground.** They all work on ink — the venue index already
carries capacity, location and Enquire in exactly this spirit.

The one thing the light ground genuinely buys is easier long-form reading, and
condition 2 of your noir approval already solved that with the lighter graphite
ground for prose surfaces.

Against that, the pivot costs the tagline on the first screen, costs the dusk
photography its best ground, and trades a distinctive look for a fashionable
one.

If you want the lightness anyway, the honest middle path is a **light venue
prose / wedding guide, dark scenes** split — but that is a third option and I
would want your say-so before building it.
